Born in 1930, right as the Great Depression began, my early years were shaped by a changing world. Though I was young during World War II, its impact was felt, and these childhood memories are filled with the wonder of a child's perspective.

When my mother became ill, a new adventure began! My aunt Edna and uncle Roy, who were my godparents, invited me to stay with them in sunny Cuba. Uncle Roy was stationed there as the military attaché for the Air Force, and I, a spirited 13-year-old, traveled by train to Miami and then flew to Havana!

My time with cousins Charlie, Joan, and Janine was absolutely wonderful! Despite the stark contrasts in Cuban society at the time, my days were brimming with joy. We explored grand avenues and vibrant streets, though I also learned about the challenges many faced.

Most of my days were pure adventure! We swam and dove at beautiful country clubs along the seashore, collecting sparkling gems from the ocean floor and inventing all sorts of games. Even school was an adventure, especially learning in Spanish! I was so proud of my English class achievements.

Thanks to my uncle's embassy connections, we socialized with fascinating people. I especially loved visiting a wealthy family's sugar plantation – it was so vast it had its own railway! Janine and I would spend glorious days riding horses, exploring with packed lunches, and feeling a wonderful sense of freedom. We often ventured towards Guantanamo, where the U.S. military base offered delightful shopping.

Eventually, it was time to return home. My journey back was a testament to my youthful independence! At just 13, I flew alone to Miami, navigated a bustling train station, and made a new friend, a kind soldier, on my two-day train trip back to New York City. I even still have the train ticket – a keepsake of an incredible solo journey!

Looking back, my time in Cuba was a truly defining experience. What started as a necessary separation blossomed into an eye-opening adventure! I saw a wider world, learned resilience, and cherished the love of my family. It was a journey that, through a child's eyes, filled me with a lasting appreciation for the world and the incredible experiences it held.
